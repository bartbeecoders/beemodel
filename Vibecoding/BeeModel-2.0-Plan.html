After v0.16.0 · v1.0 → v2.0 · Physics

BeeModel 2.0: a world that moves

The masterplan is done. This plan has two parts. First it settles the questions the masterplan left open, and that makes v1.0. Then it turns physics into a main feature, which makes v2.0. You can break any object into bricks, balls or shards that fall as rigid bodies. There are grounds and landscapes for them to land on, and gravity objects that pull things in or keep them in orbit. A particle system covers rain, sparks, water, smoke and fire. Everything stays pure assembly with no libraries, runs on every core, can be cached and scripted, and is reachable by AI agents over MCP.

BeeModel · Vibecoding/BeeModel-2.0-Plan.html · Follows the Masterplan · Written 2026-10-17 against v0.16.0

Goals

Break anything

Object › Physics › Shatter… breaks the selected object into a chosen number of pieces. The pattern can be bricks (a running bond, a mortar gap), balls (the volume packed with spheres), or shards (Voronoi cells, which can gather where it is hit). Every piece is a rigid body with its own mass. Pieces can be glued so the object breaks only when something hits it hard enough.

Ground to land on

A Ground object is an endless floor that catches shadows. A Landscape object is a heightfield you can generate, erode, sculpt and paint. Both are exact, fast colliders for rigid bodies, cloth, particles and the play-mode player. Presets: hills, mountains, canyon, dunes, and an island with a water plane.

Gravity you can place

An Attractor object pulls rigid bodies, cloth and particles towards it, or pushes them away. Its force falls off with distance, or stops at a radius. Gravity zones, vortex, wind and turbulence fields work the same way. Bodies can also attract each other (N-body), and a planet can have its own "down" for the player.

Particles, liquid, smoke, fire

An emitter on any object, with forces, collisions and lifetimes. Particles are drawn as points, sprites or instances. Liquid uses position-based fluids and is meshed into a surface. Smoke and fire run on a sparse gas grid, with combustion and blackbody flames. Volumes render in both path tracers.
Principles kept. No libraries. CPU first, with the GPU helper optional. Results are deterministic, so a bake is the same on every machine and in every co-editing session. Everything is an operator, so it has a menu entry, an undo step, a BeeScript name and an MCP tool. Anything slower than a frame runs as a job with a progress chip. Results go into the simulation cache, which has a memory budget.

Each gets an issue that writes the decision into docs/DECISIONS.md and does the small amount of work that follows from it. When they are done, v1.0.0 is tagged: the format and the APIs are stable from then on.

Stage R · The solver

Everything else stands on this: shattered walls need thousands of bodies, landscapes need mesh colliders, and balls need not to tunnel.

R1core

Substeps and a stiffer solver core

The step is split into substeps (default 4 per 1/120 s step), with "soft step" relaxation: a TGS-style solver with fewer iterations per substep. Mass ratios of 1:100 stack. Settings: rigid.world substeps N, and Properties › Physics › World.

Exit: a 20-box tower and a 10-layer brick pyramid stand still for 10 s (drift under 1 mm); a 1 kg box on a 100 kg box rests.
R2scale

10 000 bodies scale

Body and contact storage grows from the memory pools, with no fixed 1024. The broadphase becomes a dynamic BVH (incremental refit, rebuilt in parallel), replacing sweep-and-prune on X. Islands are split across workers. Instanced shapes (one hull shared by many pieces) save memory.

Exit: 10 000 spheres poured into a box: 10 s simulated in under 30 s on 8 threads; 5000 settling bricks run live at 24 fps or better.
R3static

Mesh and plane colliders collide

A passive rigid body can use its Mesh as a shape: triangles in a BVH, tested convex against triangle (GJK on each triangle, with its neighbours' normals so edges don't snag). An infinite Plane shape. Both static only. Cloth and the player already collide with meshes; they share the new BVH.

Exit: boxes and balls dropped into a bowl and onto a staircase mesh come to rest without falling through or jittering; 100k-triangle collider, 1000 bodies, live.
R4ccd

Fast bodies and collision groups collide

Continuous collision detection for small, fast bodies: conservative advancement on the swept shape, used only where a body moves more than half its size in a step. 16 collision groups, each with a mask of the groups it collides with. Per-material friction and bounce (rubber, ice, wood, metal presets).

Exit: a 1 cm ball at 100 m/s does not pass through a 1 cm wall; two groups that ignore each other pass through one another.
R5joints

Breakable joints, limits and motors joints

Every joint gets a breaking impulse; a broken joint is gone for the rest of the run, and the cache records when. Hinge limits and motors (target speed, maximum torque). A slider joint. The joint limit grows like the bodies (R2). Shatter (S4) uses these as its glue.

Exit: a chain breaks at the link that carries the most load; a motor turns a wheel at its set speed under load.
R6same

The same result everywhere quality

Order the work so that the result does not depend on the thread count: contacts sorted by body pair, islands solved independently, sums in a fixed order. Bakes match between machines and in co-editing sessions. The cache records the solver version.

Exit: the 1000-box bake is identical bit for bit with 1, 4 and 32 threads.
R7ship

Tests, docs, release v0.17.0 release

tests/solver.py with the exits above, docs/PHYSICS.md (the solver part), the CHANGELOG, the README and a benchmark. The MIT LICENSE.

Exit: ci/local.sh and GitHub CI are green; v0.17.0 is published.

Stage G · Ground & landscape

Something solid to land on: a simple floor, or terrain shaped like the real thing.

G1ground

The Ground object object

Add › Ground: an endless floor at a height, drawn with a fading grid. It catches shadows in renders (shadow-catcher mode, alpha elsewhere) and is a plane collider (R3) for rigid bodies, cloth, particles and the player. It has a physics material (friction, bounce). One per scene by default; Add › Ground on a scene that has one selects it.

Exit: a new scene, Add › Ground, a cube made a rigid body falls and rests on it; F12 shows the shadow over a transparent background.
G2terrain

The Landscape object object

A new object type: a heightfield of N × N samples (65² to 4097²) over a chosen size, stored in tiles of 64², drawn with tile LOD (far tiles coarser, seams stitched). A non-destructive stack of layers: Generate (fBm, ridged, billow, Voronoi cells, with seed and scale), Erode (the droplet erosion from procgen.s, plus thermal erosion), Terrace, Sculpted (the strokes) and Flatten area (a mask). Each layer has a strength and can be hidden.

Exit: a 1025² landscape is generated and eroded in under 1 s, edited at interactive frame rates, saved and reopened the same.
G3shape

Sculpt and paint the landscape tools

Landscape brushes in Sculpt mode: raise, lower, smooth, flatten, ramp (between two points), noise, erode (local). Paint mode paints up to 8 material layers (rock, grass, sand, snow…) into a splat map, and an auto layer rule picks them by height and slope. The splat drives the material in the viewport and in both path tracers.

Exit: fakewl paints a ramp and a grass layer with clicks; the render shows grass on the flat and rock on the slope.
G4collide

The heightfield collider collide

An exact collider that reads the heightfield directly: the cells under a shape's bounding box become triangle pairs on the fly, with nothing stored. It is used by rigid bodies, cloth, particles (P3) and the play-mode player. A physics material per paint layer, so a ball rolls further on rock than on grass.

Exit: 1000 balls rolled down a 4097² mountain stay on its surface; the player walks over it with steps no taller than the step height.
G5presets

Presets and water ui

Add › Landscape › Flat, Hills, Mountains, Canyon, Dunes, Island. Island adds a Water plane object: a flat level with a water material (transmission, IOR 1.33, ripple normals). The water plane is also a simple buoyancy zone (bodies below it float, by their submerged volume). The welcome screen's Terrain template uses Hills.

Exit: each preset is added from the menu; a wooden box dropped in the island's water floats.
G6io

Heightmaps in and out files

Import a 16-bit PNG or EXR heightmap as a landscape; export the heights and the splat map; convert a landscape to a mesh (with the current LOD, or full). World partition streams landscape tiles like its other cells.

Exit: a heightmap round-trips exactly; converting to a mesh gives the same surface within 1e-5.

Stage S · Shatter

Break any object into a number of pieces, and have every piece be physical.

Bricks

A running bond in the object's local axes: brick size or count per axis, row offset (½, ⅓, random), a mortar gap, a small random size jitter. The object is cut by the brick grid; a brick crossing the surface keeps the surface's shape.

Balls

The volume is packed with spheres: the given number of them, all one radius or a spread of radii. A dense packing is used, relaxed so balls don't overlap. The original is hidden, and each ball takes the colour of the surface nearest it. Thousands of balls share one sphere mesh (instancing).

Shards

Voronoi cells from N seed points: uniform, clustered around an impact point (the 3D cursor or a hit), or along the surface. Cells are clipped to the mesh, so concave objects work. An inner material for the new faces.
S1cut

Cutting a closed mesh into cells core

The geometry core: clip a closed mesh by a convex cell (a set of planes) and close the cut with new faces, robust to coplanar and degenerate cases. Uses the exact predicates the boolean code already has. Cells are cut in parallel. A non-manifold input is repaired first (repair.s), or refused with a reason. Mesh › Separate › By Loose Parts comes with it.

Exit: a cube, a torus and Suzanne cut into 100 cells each: every piece is closed, the volumes add up to the original's within 1e-6, and there are no slivers under 1e-5.
S2patterns

The three patterns feature

Bricks, balls and shards as above. Count, seed, size controls, gap, inner material. For balls, the packing works in the object's volume through its signed distance (the SDF tools), and a ball outside the surface is dropped.

Exit: a 2 × 1 × 0.2 m wall into 10 × 5 bricks; a sphere into 500 balls; a vase into 50 shards around a point: counts, volumes and materials as asked.
S3bodies

Every piece is a rigid body physics

Each piece becomes an active rigid body: its shape is a hull (shards and bricks are convex, so the hull is exact) or a sphere; its mass is its volume times the material's density; it starts with the object's velocity. The pieces are parented to an empty named after the original, so the outliner folds them. The original is hidden, not deleted. One undo step. object.shatter with all the options; Object › Physics › Shatter… is its form.

Exit: shatter a cube into 200 shards, press play: they fall and settle on the ground; Ctrl+Z gives the cube back.
S4glue

Glue: break on impact physics

Neighbouring pieces (those that share a face) get a glue joint (R5) with a strength. A glued object behaves as one body until a contact impulse breaks the joints near it; the pieces that come loose start moving. Strength can vary: weaker towards the impact point, or painted with weight paint.

Exit: a ball thrown at a glued brick wall knocks out a hole the size of the ball and the rest stands; a weaker throw leaves it standing.
S5live

Shatter while it runs play

An object can be marked Shatter on impact: it stays whole and is cut only when hit above a threshold, around the point of the hit, during the simulation and in play mode. The cutting is precomputed when the run starts, so it costs no frame time. Logic node shatter.

Exit: in play mode, a ball shot at a vase breaks it where it hits, with no frame over 16 ms.
S6ui

The Shatter form and panel ui

A form with a live preview of the cells (drawn as outlines before anything is cut), presets (Brick wall, Glass, Stone, Gravel balls), and a Shatter section in Properties › Physics for re-cutting with other settings. BeeScript shatter(name, pattern, count [, options]).

Exit: a fakewl test shatters a wall from the menu, changes the count in the panel and runs it, with clicks only.

Stage A · Attractors & force fields

Gravity becomes something you place. One field system pushes rigid bodies, cloth and particles.

A1fields

Force field objects object

A new object type, Add › Force Field ›:

  • Attractor: pulls towards its centre, by inverse square (softened near the centre) or linear; a negative strength repels.
  • Gravity zone: a box or sphere inside which "down" is a different vector.
  • Wind: a direction, with gusts.
  • Vortex: turns things around its axis.
  • Turbulence: curl noise.
  • Drag.

Every field has a falloff (none, radius, smooth) and a strength that can be keyframed. The viewport draws the icon and the radius. A Fields section in Properties › Physics.

Exit: each type moves a test body the way it should, within 1% of the analytic force.
A2apply

Fields on everything physics

Rigid bodies (at their centre of mass), cloth and soft bodies (per particle) and particles (P1) take the sum of the fields. A field can apply to some collision groups only. The world's gravity can be switched off for space scenes. Integration is symplectic (velocity first), so orbits keep their energy.

Exit: a body in a circular orbit around an attractor keeps its radius within 1% over 10 orbits; a tablecloth blows in the wind.
A3nbody

Bodies that attract each other physics

A rigid body can be marked Attracts: it pulls every other body by its mass, with a G you scale. Barnes–Hut (an octree of masses) keeps this fast for thousands of bodies. Shatter a moon and the pieces orbit and clump.

Exit: two bodies orbit their common centre; 5000 attracting bodies run at 10 steps per second or better on 8 threads.
A4planet

Walk on a planet play

In play mode the player's "down" follows the strongest field where it stands, and the camera turns with it. A sphere with an attractor becomes a small planet to walk around. Logic nodes: field (sample the force at a point) and set strength.

Exit: the player walks once round a 20 m planet and comes back to its start.
A5ui

Seeing the fields ui

A viewport overlay draws the field as arrows on a grid or as streamlines from the selected object. Presets: Planet, Black hole (strong and short), Tornado (vortex and lift), Breeze. BeeScript field(type, name, …), and force(x, y, z) to sample the total.

Exit: fakewl adds a tornado from the menu; the overlay shows its swirl.

Stage P · Particles & liquid

One particle core, used for simple effects and as the basis of the liquid solver.

P1core

The particle system core

A particle system belongs to an emitter object. It emits from faces, the volume, vertices or a point; at a rate or all at once; with a lifetime, a start speed along the normal or a direction, and random spread. Particles are stored per field (structure of arrays: position, velocity, age, size, colour, id) in pooled blocks, up to 4 million. Forces come from gravity and the fields (A2), plus drag. Steps run in parallel chunks.

Exit: 1M particles step at under 10 ms on 8 threads; a fountain's arc matches the ballistic curve within 1e-3.
P2draw

Drawing and rendering particles render

The viewport draws particles as points or camera-facing sprites, or as instances of up to 8 objects (debris, leaves, raindrops), through inst.s. Colour and size can change over the lifetime (a ramp). Hive and the GPU helper render them as spheres, or as the instanced meshes.

Exit: 1M points draw at 60 fps; a render of 100k instanced leaves matches between the CPU and the GPU.
P3collide

Collisions and events physics

Particles hit meshes, the ground, landscapes and rigid bodies (a hash grid on the particle side, the BVHs on the other), with bounce, friction, or dying, sticking or splitting on a hit. Events (born, hit, died) can emit a second system: sparks that throw sparks, rain that splashes.

Exit: rain on a landscape: no particle passes through; every hit makes a splash.
P4liquid

Liquid solver

Position-based fluids: density constraints solved over neighbours found in a spatial hash, with XSPH viscosity, vorticity confinement and surface tension. It runs on the particle core, uses the same colliders, and moves rigid bodies in both directions (things float and get pushed). Settings: particle size, viscosity (water, oil, honey), domain box.

Exit: a dam break of 200k particles bakes at under 1 s per frame on 8 threads; the water level at rest is flat within one particle radius; a box floats.
P5surface

The liquid's surface render

Each frame the particles are turned into a mesh: a smoothed density field on a sparse grid, then marching cubes (the SDF code meshes this way already). It has a water material, with foam and spray as a secondary particle system where the surface breaks. The mesh can be exported per frame (OBJ sequence, or glTF with morph targets).

Exit: the dam break renders as a closed, smooth surface with no holes, in under 200 ms per frame for meshing.
P6ui

The Particles tab and presets ui

A new Properties tab, Particles: the systems on the object, emission, motion, render, collisions, liquid. Presets: Rain, Snow, Sparks, Confetti, Fountain, Water tank. Cached in the simulation cache (channel 2 particles, 3 liquid), with positions quantized to 16 bits inside the domain. BeeScript emit(object, preset) and particles(k).

Exit: fakewl adds a fountain with clicks; the bake fits the cache budget; save and reopen plays the same frames.

Stage V · Smoke, fire & volumes

A gas solver on a sparse grid, and volumes in both path tracers.

V1gas

The smoke solver solver

An Eulerian grid in 8³ bricks, with only the active bricks allocated, and a domain that follows the smoke. Steps:

  • Semi-Lagrangian advection (MacCormack for detail).
  • Buoyancy from temperature.
  • Vorticity confinement.
  • A multigrid pressure solve, so the flow is divergence-free.
  • Colliders block the flow, and moving ones push it.

Fields: density, temperature, velocity. Sources: a mesh, a particle system, or an emitter shape.

Exit: a smoke plume at 128³ effective bakes in under 0.5 s per frame on 8 threads; smoke flows around a sphere, not through it.
V2fire

Fire solver

Combustion on the gas grid: fuel burns into heat and smoke at a rate. A flame field drives emission, coloured by temperature through the blackbody node the materials already have. Presets: Campfire, Candle, Torch, Explosion (a burst of fuel and an outward push). Logic node ignite.

Exit: a campfire burns steadily for 10 s; an explosion expands and fades; both look right in a render.
V3render

Volumes in Hive and on the GPU render

Participating media in both path tracers:

  • Delta tracking for sampling and ratio tracking for shadows, over the brick grid (a 3D DDA through the bricks).
  • Absorption, scattering (Henyey–Greenstein) and emission.
  • A Volume material output for any mesh (fog in a box, fog over the landscape), as well as gas grids.

The CPU and the GPU must give the same image, as tests/gpu.py already checks for surfaces.

Exit: a smoke render matches between the CPU and the GPU (mean difference under 0.5 levels at 256 samples); a fog box over the ground shows god rays from the sun.
V4view

Volumes in the viewport viewport

Solid and Material Preview raymarch the density (one light, low steps) so smoke and fire show while you work; Rendered uses V3. A slice view for debugging (density, temperature, velocity).

Exit: a 128³ plume draws in the viewport at 30 fps or better.
V5io

Caching and export files

Gas grids are cached as sparse bricks, quantized to 8 or 16 bits per field (cache channel 4). Export as OpenVDB (our own writer of the format's grid layout, with no library) so Blender and others can render it.

Exit: a 10 s plume fits the default 1 GB budget; the exported .vdb opens in Blender with the same density.

  • CPU vs GPU images for particles and volumes, in tests/gpu.py.
  • The UI with clicks only in tools/fakewl.py, for each stage (shatter, landscape, fields, particles, smoke).
  • New files: tests/solver.py, tests/landscape.py, tests/shatter.py, tests/fields.py, tests/particles.py, tests/gas.py, tests/physics2.py. Each is added to ci/local.sh and GitHub CI.

Budgets & risks

Budgets (8 threads)

  • Rigid: 10 000 spheres, 10 s simulated in under 30 s; 5000 bricks live at 24 fps or better
  • Shatter: 500 cells from a 100k-triangle mesh in under 1 s
  • Landscape: 1025² generated and eroded in under 1 s; edits at 60 fps
  • Particles: 1M stepped in under 10 ms
  • Liquid: 200k particles, under 1 s per frame plus 200 ms meshing
  • Gas: 128³ effective, under 0.5 s per frame
  • Binary: under 2 MB at v2.0 (it is 1.27 MB today)

Risks

  • Speed of the solvers in pure assembly. Write the hot loops in AVX2 from the start, measure each budget in CI, and keep the GPU helper as a way out for liquid and gas.
  • Robust cutting. Degenerate cuts are where fracture tools fail. Reuse the boolean code's exact predicates, and fuzz it.
  • Stability with many contacts. Substeps and soft steps (R1) come before shatter. Glue only between neighbours.
  • Cache size. Liquid and gas are big. Quantize, keep only active bricks, and say clearly when the budget is reached.
