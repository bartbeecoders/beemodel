Stage A of the 2.0 plan (Vibecoding/BeeModel-2.0-Plan.html): attractors and force fields, issues A1-A6 (#329-#334). docs/FIELDS.md.
Everything in this release

Added

  • Force field objects (A1, #329): Add › Force Field › Attractor (strength / distance squared, softened near its centre, or strength / distance; a negative strength pushes), Gravity Zone (a box or sphere whose down is its -Y), Wind (along its +X, with gusts), Vortex (turning round its +Y, with lift and inflow), Turbulence (curl noise) and Drag. Each has a falloff (none, radius, smooth), a strength that keys (field:Name:strength), the collision groups it acts on, and an on/off switch. They draw a gizmo of their kind and their reach. A Force Field section in Properties › Physics, field.add_<type> and field.set, BeeScript field() and force(x, y, z). Fields undo, save (a FLDS chunk) and copy with a duplicate.
  • Fields on everything (A2, #330): rigid bodies feel the sum at their centre of mass each substep (velocity first, so a circular orbit keeps its radius within 0.1% over ten turns); cloth and soft bodies per particle, with wind fields as air for each triangle. World › Gravity switches the world's gravity off for space scenes. Changing a field runs the simulation again (the cache's key).
  • Bodies that attract each other (A3, #331): Attracts the others on a rigid body (rigid.add attracts), G in World (rigid.world g). A Barnes-Hut octree once a step, the pulls across the workers, given as a leapfrog kick: two bodies orbit their common centre within 0.04%; 5445 attracting bodies run at about 500 steps a second (physbench nbody). rbinfo(22-24).
  • Walk on a planet (A4, #332): in play mode the player's up follows the pull where it stands and the camera turns with it, so walking round a sphere with an attractor brings you back to where you started. Logic nodes Set Strength and Field (the pull at a point). playinfo(16-18): the player's up.
  • Seeing the fields (A5, #333): View › Force Field Arrows (the pull on a grid round the fields) and View › Force Field Lines (streamlines from the selected objects). Presets Planet (a sphere with its core, the world's gravity off), Black Hole, Tornado and Breeze, also as field("tornado"). fieldinfo(k).

Fixed

  • A new armature's colour no longer comes from past the end of the per-type colour table.

v0.19.0

18 October 2026
Download · 1.3 MBRelease pageSource
"Shatter": stage S of the 2.0 plan (Vibecoding/BeeModel-2.0-Plan.html, planned as v1.2.0; released before stage D's v1.0.0), issues S1-S7 (#309-#315). How it works: docs/SHATTER.md, docs/PHYSICS.md; the benchmark: tools/physbench.py (shatter).
Everything in this release

Added

  • Cutting a closed mesh into cells (#309): object.shatter. Each cell (a set of planes) is clipped from the mesh in doubles, and each plane's hole is filled at once, so every piece is closed. The holes are filled by bridging, ear clipping and merging back into convex polygons. A tree of cells cuts big meshes once near each cell, in parallel. Results: — the pieces' volumes add up to the original's within 1e-6, with no slivers; — 500 cells of a 49 000-triangle vase take about 54 ms; — an open mesh is repaired first. mesh.separate_loose (Mesh › Separate) splits a mesh into its loose parts.
  • The three patterns (#310): — shards: Voronoi cells from seeds inside the mesh, spread evenly, gathered around a point (at, cursor) or near the surface; — bricks: a running bond in the object's axes, set by size or counts, with offset, a mortar gap and jitter; — balls: packed on a face-centred cubic lattice inside the mesh, none overlapping. inner r g b colours the cut faces. Each piece keeps the original's material.
  • Every piece a rigid body (#311). Each piece is an active convex-hull body whose mass is its volume × the density (from density, else the material). Its centre of mass and inertia come from the hull itself. The pieces are children of the hidden original, and one undo step brings the original back. Piles of shards settle and sleep.
  • Glue (#312): glue N/m2 [paint]. A fixed joint ties every two pieces whose cut faces meet and breaks at the strength × the shared area. Joints now break inside the solve, so a 30 kg ball at 20 m/s knocks a hole the size of the ball in a glued brick wall and the rest stands. Up to 16 384 joints are allowed (ANIM v3 saves only those in use).
  • Shatter while it runs (#313): impact N s. The object stays whole until a blow that hard, then breaks in the same step, where it was hit. Its pieces are made and glued beforehand, so no frame takes over 16 ms. A logic graph's Shatter node breaks it on demand. The cache keeps which objects are broken. New rbinfo keys 17-21 report the fastest body, joint rows and impact groups.
  • The Shatter form and panel (#314): — Object › Physics › Shatter... opens a form that draws the cells as outlines on the object while it is open; — presets: Brick wall, Glass, Stone, Gravel balls (preset); — a Shatter section in Properties › Physics to re-cut with other settings (again); — BeeScript shatter(name, pattern, count [, options]), shinfo(k), get(name, "hidden"), and rigid(name, "x0") / "y0" / "z0".
  • tests/shatter.py checks all of it, the form and the panel with clicks in the fake compositor. tools/physbench.py times a 99 072-triangle vase cut into 500 shards: 455 / 163 / 121 / 105 ms on 1 / 4 / 8 / 32 threads.

Changed

  • Seeds and balls are tested inside the mesh through a grid of its triangles, as seen along the test ray: 250 ms became 12 ms on the 100 000-triangle vase.
  • Hidden objects are not rigid bodies, except originals that wait to shatter.
  • The joint list in Properties › Physics shows its first 6 joints.

Notes

  • Balls are not cut: they are spheres that fit inside the mesh, and the preview draws nothing for them.
  • A lone thin wedge-shaped shard can rock on the ground for a long time before it sleeps.

Everything in this release

Added

  • The Ground (#301): Add › Ground (create.ground [height h]), an endless floor, one a scene. The viewport draws it as a grid that fades with the distance from the view's centre. Renders make it a shadow catcher: the film has alpha (no sky behind objects, the shadows caught on the ground); the render window shows it over checks, and render.save and render() write RGBA PNGs. It is a passive plane rigid body (friction 0.5), a cloth collider, and the player walks on it.
  • Landscapes (#302): Add › Landscape, landscape.add. A heightfield of 65 to 4097 samples a side over a chosen size, kept with its mesh (undo, copies, saving) and built from a stack of layers that stays editable: generate (fBm, ridged, billow, Voronoi cells), erode (droplets, then thermal), terrace, sculpted, flatten (round areas), heightmap; each with a strength, hidden or shown (landscape.layer add|set|remove|hide|show; landscape.set size|res|detail). A 1025 x 1025 landscape is generated and eroded in about 0.2 s, and a layer above the erosion changes in about 4 ms (the erosion's result is cached). landscape(name, k), height(name, x, z).
  • Sculpting and painting landscapes (#303). Sculpt mode on a landscape has its own tools, at its full resolution: raise, lower, smooth, flatten, ramp (a straight slope from where the stroke begins to where it ends), noise and erode, into a Sculpted layer; a stroke is one undo step. Paint layers (rock, grass, sand, snow, dirt, gravel, moss, mud: a colour, friction, bounce and a height and slope rule) are painted with clicks or by Auto Paint (rock on the slopes, grass on the flat ...); the blend shows in the viewport and in both path tracers. landscape.brush, landscape.paint.
  • The heightfield collider (#304). A landscape made a passive mesh body is not cut into triangles: each step, the cells under whatever moves become triangles on the fly, in slots kept while in use, and contacts work as on a mesh. Each paint layer has its own friction and bounce (a box slides further on rock than on grass). Cloth lands on landscapes by their heights. 1000 balls roll down a 4097 x 4097 mountain, none under its surface, 10 s simulated in 2.4 s.
  • Presets and water (#305): Add › Landscape › Flat, Hills, Mountains, Canyon, Dunes, Island. The island comes with its sea: a Water plane (the water material, rippled) that floats what is lighter than water - a wooden box floats, a stone one sinks - and that the player does not walk on. Generate gains island and offset. The welcome screen's Terrain template is a Hills landscape.
  • Heightmaps in and out (#306): landscape.import (16-bit or 8-bit PNG, grey or colour; OpenEXR scanlines, half or float, uncompressed or ZIP), landscape.export (16-bit grey PNG - a PNG made so round-trips exactly - float EXR, or the paint as RGBA PNGs), landscape.to_mesh [full], and landscape.tiles k, which splits a landscape into k x k landscapes that world partition streams (its cell files keep them as landscapes).
  • tests/landscape.py checks all of it (with clicks on the fake compositor for the brushes, the paint and the menus); tools/physbench.py times the mountain.

Notes

  • A landscape is drawn at up to 513 samples a side (every 2nd, 4th ... sample of a bigger one); the tile LOD drawing the plan names is not done.
  • Landscape painting lives in its sculpt mode's tools, not in Paint mode.

v0.17.0

17 October 2026
Download · 1.2 MBRelease pageSource
"The solver": stage R of the 2.0 plan (Vibecoding/BeeModel-2.0-Plan.html), issues R1-R7 (#286-#292). How it works: docs/PHYSICS.md; the benchmark: tools/physbench.py.
Everything in this release

Added

  • The rigid-body solver takes each step in substeps (#286, "soft step"): 4 by default (rigid.world substeps n, Properties › Physics › World › Substeps), with soft contacts that push overlaps apart as a damped spring (twice as stiff against static bodies) and a relax pass, restitution once at the end. Contacts between boxes and hulls are found by clipping their faces (src/clip.s): a box resting on another touches at its four corners. A tower of 20 boxes and a 10-layer brick pyramid stand (they used to topple), and a 100 kg box rests on a 1 kg one. tests/solver.py.
  • 10 000 rigid bodies (#287). Instances are bodies: rigid.add ... instances (Properties › Physics › Bodies) makes each instance of an object's geometry nodes a body shaped like its prototype; they move in the viewport and the renders, are baked into the cache and played back. Up to 4096 objects can be bodies (was 1024; files save only those in use and older files open). The broad phase is a hash grid, pairs are looked up in parallel in a hash kept between steps, and a big island's contacts are coloured and solved across the threads (the same result on any number of them). Contacts start further apart for fast bodies, so small ones no longer pass through each other; spheres collide exactly. 10 800 balls poured into a box: 10 s simulated in about 26 s on 8 threads (4000 took 23 s before); 5000 bricks settle at about 27 fps; 1000 tumbling boxes in 2.7 s (was 6.2). instpos(object, i, k); rbinfo(8..14).
  • Mesh and plane colliders (#288). A passive rigid body can be its mesh - any shape, each triangle a collider, found through a tree - or an endless plane (rigid.add passive mesh, passive plane; Properties › Physics › shape). Edges inside a flat surface don't catch: a contact there takes the face's normal, so a box slides over a ramp of thousands of triangles without a bump. Balls and boxes in a bowl, boxes on a staircase, 1000 balls on a terrain of 100 000 triangles at about 50 fps on 8 threads, a box landing on a plane 500 m out.
  • Fast bodies, groups and materials (#289). A body that moves more than half its size in a step is stopped short of static bodies by continuous collision, and contacts start as far apart as two bodies can close in a step: a 1 cm ball at 300 m/s and a tumbling box at 60 m/s stop at a 1 cm wall. Collision groups: rigid.add ... group n collide m (16 groups; m the groups it meets). Physics materials: material rubber|ice|wood|metal|stone and Properties › Physics › Material. A starting velocity: rigid.add ... velocity x y z.
  • Breakable joints, limits, motors and sliders (#290). rigid.joint ... break f breaks a joint whose load passes f newtons (it is gone for the rest of the run; rbinfo(15) counts them); limits lo hi stops a hinge (degrees) or a slider (metres) there; motor speed force turns a hinge (degrees a second) or moves a slider (metres a second) with at most that torque or force. A new slider joint keeps two bodies on a line, turning together (Add Joint › Type › Slider).
  • The same result on any number of threads (#291): 1000 tumbling boxes, 5000 bricks and the joint tests end bit for bit the same on 1, 4, 8 and 32 threads; rbinfo(16) hashes every body's state to show it.
  • The MIT License (LICENSE, also in the release archive). The 2.0 plan (Vibecoding/BeeModel-2.0-Plan.html): the masterplan's open questions, then physics - the solver, ground and landscapes, shatter, attractors, particles, smoke and fire.

Fixed

  • Rigid bodies: a body given an impulse when play began (Logic › Apply Impulse) did not move - the bodies were made again on the next frame. Continuous collision skips a body touching what it is checked against where its step began (it rests or slides on it).
  • Rigid bodies: a thin passive box (under 5 cm) was made 25 cm deep on every thin axis - meant for floors, it also thickened thin walls towards whatever came at them. Only a floor's own Y now; a passive body with no bounce no longer stops what comes at it short of it.
  • Sculpting: each thread's sums for a dab (the area's weight, centre and normal) get a cache line of their own. Two threads shared one, and how much that cost depended on where the linker put them (a brush frame took 4.1 ms instead of 2.6 when unrelated data moved).
  • parallel_for: a worker still leaving one call could take a chunk of the next - with the old chunk size, or past the old count - and part of it ran twice (a crash, or a different result, when many calls with different sizes ran back to back: the rigid-body steps). A chunk is now claimed by compare-and-swap on one word that holds the call's generation and the next index; the caller closes the last call's claim before it writes the next. The self-test runs 3000 calls back to back.

First release: the application shell that the 3D modeling tool will be built on.
Everything in this release

Added

  • Native Wayland client in pure x86-64 assembly with no libraries: raw syscalls and a hand-written wire protocol (wl_compositor, wl_shm, xdg_wm_base, wl_seat, wp_cursor_shape_v1).
  • Title bar drawn by the app: move by dragging, double-click to maximize, minimize/maximize/close buttons, edge resizing for floating windows.
  • Menu system with File, Edit, View, Create and Help dropdowns, hover switching, check marks and shortcut hints.
  • Resizable Tools, Outliner/Properties and Console panes with draggable splitters; each pane can be shown or hidden from the View menu.
  • 3D viewport canvas: orbit/pan/zoom camera, perspective projection with near-plane and Liang–Barsky clipping, ground grid, axes, orientation gizmo and a demo cube.
  • Status bar, console log, keyboard shortcuts (Q/W/E/R, G, F, Esc, Ctrl+Q).
  • --version and --help command-line flags.
  • Event-driven rendering paced by frame callbacks: 0% CPU when idle, double-buffered shared-memory framebuffer, about 60 KB of private memory.
  • build.sh, run.sh and package.sh scripts.
  • Headless test harness (fake Wayland compositor) and GitHub Actions CI and release pipelines.
